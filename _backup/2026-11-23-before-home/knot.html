    What makes it hold

    • Two wraps first. The extra friction of a double first throw holds the wound closed while the second throw is prepared — this is what makes it a surgeon’s knot rather than a plain one.
    • Reverse the second throw. The same direction twice gives a granny knot, which slides as the wound swells.
    • Pull along the wound line, evenly. Both strands the same distance in opposite directions; stop when the edges meet, not when the skin blanches.
    • Regrasp close to the knot for every throw. The nearer the grip, the finer the control of tension.
    ⓘ Teaching model, not anatomy. The tissue is a diagram of a skin incision drawn to scale in millimetres; instruments are simplified. The sequence and the tension rules are the teaching point.
